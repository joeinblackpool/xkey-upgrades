A dumpling squishy from a known brand and a proper shop is generally a low-risk toy for children over 3, but the craze has brought real problems. In October 2026 the US recalled about 37,000 cheap "Squishy Dumpling" toys because the water beads inside were too big when swollen, and UK regulators have recalled dumplings with benzene in their outer layer. Copies with a strong chemical smell, toys that split, and a social media trend of microwaving squishies are the other main risks. The safest approach is to buy from a shop you can trace, check the label, smell the toy, and throw away anything damaged or smelly.

*This page is general information, not medical advice. If you think a child has swallowed part of a toy, in the UK call NHS 111, or 999 if they are choking or struggling to breathe; in the US call Poison Control on 1-800-222-1222, or 911 in an emergency, or speak to a doctor.*

## The October 2026 US recall

The US Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) announced recall 27-003 on 1 October 2026.

| Detail | What the CPSC says |
|---|---|
| Products | Squishy Dumpling toys, model 3655; Crazy Squib Ball toys, model 3984 |
| Importer | ABC Trading Inc., Vernon, California (made in China) |
| Units | About 37,000 |
| How to recognise the dumpling | Translucent, in several colours, with glitter and water beads inside and a cartoon face; sold in a tan plastic pot shaped like a bamboo steamer with a removable "SQUISHY DUMPLING" label |
| Problem | Water beads inside expand larger than the mandatory toy standard allows; if swallowed they can cause choking or a blocked intestine, with a risk of death to a child |
| Injuries reported | None |
| Sold | ABC Trading stores in Los Angeles and other stores nationwide, April to June 2026, for 41 cents to $1.25 |

**What to do:** stop using the toys straight away. For a full refund, the CPSC says to write "RECALLED" on each toy in permanent marker, throw it away, and email a photo of the disposed toy to ABC Trading at recallabc@gmail.com.

The recalled dumpling has no markings apart from its face, so if you bought a very cheap one in a steamer-style pot in the US in spring 2026 with beads and glitter inside, check it against the table.

### Other dumpling recalls
The CPSC's water beads page lists two more water bead recalls of the same kind of toy: LLK Trading's Mochi Malrang Mystery Dumpling toys and Toyworld168's Rainbow Mystery Squishy Bun toys. According to WFTV, the LLK recall covered about 480 translucent red dumplings sold at Easy Wholesale in San Jose, California, in July 2026 for about $10.

## Water beads: why they matter

Water beads are small balls of super-absorbent polymer. The CPSC says they can grow up to 100 times their size in water; the UK's Office for Product Safety and Standards (OPSS) says up to 400 times. Once swallowed, they can keep growing and block the gut, which can need surgery. OPSS adds that they do not show up on X-ray, which makes them hard to find. The CPSC counted nearly 7,000 emergency department visits in the US for swallowed water beads from 2018 to 2022, and knows of at least one death, a 10-month-old girl in 2023.

### US rules
- A federal safety standard for water beads in toys took effect on **12 March 2026**, according to the CPSC.
- The Toy Association says it covers toys that contain water beads, including squeeze or squishy balls with beads sealed inside, and sets a maximum size of 5 mm once a bead is fully swollen. It also limits acrylamide, a chemical the CPSC calls a known carcinogen.
- The recalled ABC Trading toys were sold after the rule took effect.
- A separate bill, Esther's Law, introduced in the Senate in May 2024, would ban water beads marketed to children outright. We could not confirm that it has become law.

### UK rules
We could not find a UK ban on water beads. OPSS issued a safety alert in September 2024 after removing unsafe water bead products, and Which? says some dumpling versions contain water beads that fall under that alert. OPSS advice is to keep water beads away from children under 5 and to supervise older children and vulnerable adults closely.

## Chemicals and fakes in the UK

- On 25 June 2026 OPSS recalled "Squeezy Dumplings" sold by Samsons Cash and Carry Ltd of Cardiff, rating the risk as serious. Testing found benzene in the outer layer, the product did not meet the Toys (Safety) Regulations 2011, and it had no batch numbers. Benzene can irritate the eyes, nose and throat.
- Which? reports that more than 1,000 of these were seized from shops in Hull in July.
- Rhondda Cynon Taf Council collected 224 unsafe squishies from traders in July 2026, many missing the maker's details, safety marks, batch codes and warnings. Some were suspected counterfeits.
- Cornwall Council said in August 2026 that hundreds had been removed from sale there, mostly because labels lacked required safety information.

In the US, KSL reported industry concerns that some squishies were packed before the material had fully cured, raising benzene worries, and the CPSC warned in August 2026 that counterfeit squishies may contain lead, banned phthalates or unknown fillings.

## Choking and small parts

Squishies can split or tear. When they do, the filling and any beads, glitter or gel inside can come out. Smyths Toys warns on its Crazy Fun dumpling listing that it contains small parts, is a choking hazard and is not suitable for children under 3. Rhondda Cynon Taf Council points out that toys that look like food are more likely to end up in a young child's mouth.

## Heat and the microwave trend

The CPSC, Which? and Suffolk County Council have all warned about videos showing children microwaving squishies to soften them. Heated squishies can burst and the hot, sticky filling clings to skin. Suffolk's alert mentions a 10-year-old scalded on the face. Smyths' product page says not to heat squishies or put them in a microwave. The CPSC also warns that squishies left in sunlight or hot cars can get dangerously hot. Which? adds that coating them in nail varnish can make them swell and burst.

## What parents should check

Before buying:

- The maker's or importer's name and a contact address. In the UK, look for a UK importer or EU manufacturer.
- A UKCA or CE mark (UK). Rhondda Cynon Taf Council says a mark alone is not enough; everything on this list should be there.
- An age warning and safety instructions.
- A batch code.
- A price that is not far below the established shops; the CPSC lists this as a sign of a fake.

After opening:

- Throw it away if it has a strong chemical, solvent or petrol-like smell.
- Stop using it if it is torn, leaking, sticky, oily, discoloured or unusually soft.
- Check whether it contains beads. If you are in the US, compare it with the recall details above.
- Keep it away from children under 3 and never heat it.

## If a child swallows part of a squishy

Do not wait for symptoms. Poison Control says a single water bead can cause a serious blockage, and in one case a toddler's symptoms started about 15 hours later.

**UK:** NHS guidance for swallowed objects says to call 999 or go to A&E if a child is choking, struggling to breathe, drooling, gagging or vomiting, refusing food, in severe pain, or very sleepy. For milder signs, or if you are worried, call NHS 111 or contact a doctor the same day. OPSS says to get medical help straight away if a water bead is swallowed.

**US:** call Poison Control on 1-800-222-1222 (free and available 24 hours a day) or use its online tool. If a child is choking, call 911. The CPSC also says to seek medical treatment straight away if a child swallows a water bead or puts one in an ear or nose.

For a burn from a heated squishy, the CPSC advises running cool water over it for at least 20 minutes, not using ice, covering it with a non-stick dressing, and getting medical help for severe burns or burns on young children.

## How to report an unsafe squishy

- **UK:** Citizens Advice consumer helpline, 0808 223 1133, which passes reports to Trading Standards (Cornwall Council and Suffolk County Council both give this number).
- **US:** SaferProducts.gov, run by the CPSC.

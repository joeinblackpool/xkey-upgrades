Peekimo is the simplest and most "pet-like" of the current virtual pets: a furry creature in a pod with no screen, five games and a mood light, for about £24.99 or $24.99. Tamagotchi Uni and Tamagotchi Paradise are screen-based, with far more characters and features, and cost more. Bitzee, also from Spin Master, sits in the middle. For a younger child (it is rated 5+) who wants something cute to carry around, Peekimo is a sensible, cheap choice. For an older child who wants to raise and collect characters for months, a Tamagotchi will offer more.

## How they differ

### Screen vs no screen
This is the biggest difference. Peekimo's pet is a physical furry figure that pops out of its pod, and it shows feelings through a coloured light and sounds. Smyths and Target both describe it as screen-free. Tamagotchis use a small LCD screen. Bitzee is in between: FOX 9 described the original as a 3D-looking pet made from colour-changing LED strips inside a shell.

If you are trying to cut screen time, Peekimo is the clearest choice. If your child likes the detail of a pixel-art pet and menus, Peekimo may feel basic.

### Depth of play
- **Peekimo:** feed, cuddle, talk, read eight moods, unlock five games through birthdays, and meet other Peekimos. That is the whole loop.
- **Tamagotchi Paradise:** a dial zooms from a cell up to outer space, there is a shopping-style Lab Mode, characters can be bred to pass on traits, and new lands can be unlocked.
- **Tamagotchi Uni:** Wi-Fi play through the Tamaverse, with arenas, parties, fashion and travel, plus downloadable content and updates.
- **Bitzee Magicals:** grow pets from baby to adult to "Super Bitzee", collect up to 20, and earn potions in games.

Peekimo will be quicker to master. That suits younger children but may mean it is put away sooner.

### Wearability
Peekimo and Tamagotchi Uni are the two designed to be worn: Peekimo on a lanyard or carabiner, the Uni on an included wristband. Bitzee is handheld and the Aquarium is meant to sit on a desk.

### Batteries and running costs
Peekimo uses one AAA, the cheapest to run here. Bitzee uses three AA (included). Tamagotchi Paradise needs two AAA that are not in the box, according to HMV and Walmart, so add those to the gift. None of the listings mention subscriptions or in-app purchases.

### Playing with friends
Peekimo's social feature is simple: two units close together react to each other. Tamagotchi Paradise connects with a friend's device to see whether characters get on, and the Uni goes online over Wi-Fi. If your child's friends already have Tamagotchis, a Peekimo will not connect with them.

## Safety notes

All of these have small parts and carry choking warnings for under-3s on the listings we read. Target's Bitzee Magicals listing also warns that flashing lights may trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy. Peekimo and Tamagotchi Paradise use standard AAA batteries and Bitzee uses AA, not coin or button cells, but batteries should still be changed by an adult and kept away from small children.

## Is Peekimo worth it?

**Probably yes if:**
- your child is at the younger end of the age range (5+) and likes cute, cuddly things;
- you want a gift without a screen;
- you want something under £25 or $25 with the battery included;
- a friend or sibling is also getting one, so the two can interact.

**Probably not if:**
- your child is older or already loves Tamagotchi and wants more to do;
- they want to collect lots of different characters (Bitzee or Tamagotchi Paradise give more);
- they want online features (Tamagotchi Uni).

One caution: Peekimo is new. The only independent review we found (People of Play, August 2026) listed no downsides, and retailer ratings were based on a few dozen reviews. How long children stay interested is not yet known.

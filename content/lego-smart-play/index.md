LEGO SMART Play is a system of electronic LEGO pieces that make models light up and make sounds as children play with them. The heart of it is the **Smart Brick**, an ordinary-looking 2x4 brick with motion, colour and light sensors, LEDs and a small speaker inside. It reacts to special **Smart Tags** and **Smart Minifigures**, so a TIE Fighter makes engine noises when you swoop it and Pikachu reacts when you "feed" it. LEGO announced it at CES in January 2026, the first sets went on sale on 1 March 2026, and Pokémon sets followed on 1 August. You cannot buy the Smart Brick on its own from LEGO: it only comes in "All-in-One" sets, which start at £59.99 in the UK and $69.99 in the US.

This page covers how the system works, why interest is growing, who it suits, what it costs and the drawbacks worth knowing before you buy.

## How SMART Play works

There are three kinds of smart piece:

| Piece | What it is | Needs charging? |
|---|---|---|
| Smart Brick | A 2x4 brick with an accelerometer, colour and light sensing, an LED array, a speaker and a sound synthesiser | Yes, on a wireless charger |
| Smart Tag | A flat 2x2 tile with a unique digital ID that tells the brick what it is part of (a ship, a throne, a sandwich) | No |
| Smart Minifigure | Looks like a normal minifigure but contains a chip with its own ID | No |

When a tag or minifigure is close to the brick, the brick reads it through near-field magnetic communication, a short-range method similar to contactless cards. It then plays matching lights and sounds. The sounds are generated on the brick in real time rather than played from recorded clips, which is why reviewers describe them as synthesised rather than film-accurate. Two or more bricks can find and talk to each other over a Bluetooth-based link LEGO calls BrickNet, with no hub, app or controller needed.

Play does not need a screen. There is an optional **LEGO SMART Assist** app for adults that shows battery level, changes volume and installs firmware updates. Our guide to [the Smart Brick's battery, charger and app](/lego-smart-play/lego-smart-brick-battery-charger-app/) covers this in detail.

### All-in-One vs Compatible sets

This is the most important thing to understand before buying:

- **All-in-One** sets include at least one Smart Brick, the Smart Charger, and smart tags or figures. They work straight out of the box.
- **Compatible** sets include only Smart Tags and Smart Minifigures (or Pokémon figures). On their own they are normal LEGO sets. To get lights and sounds you need a Smart Brick from an All-in-One set.

Of the 20 SMART Play sets listed on LEGO's US site in October 2026, only five are All-in-One: three Star Wars and two Pokémon. The full list with prices is in our [SMART Play sets and prices guide](/lego-smart-play/lego-smart-play-sets-and-prices/).

## Why interest is rising

SMART Play is new: nothing like it existed before January 2026, and LEGO called it its "most revolutionary innovation" in nearly 50 years, according to the BBC's report from CES. Several things since then point to growing interest rather than a launch spike:

- **The range has grown fast.** It launched with eight Star Wars sets in March and added 12 Pokémon sets plus a promotional set in August, according to Stonewars' pre-order list.
- **More is coming.** LEGO released a teaser for 2027 Pokémon SMART Play sets at the Pokémon XP fan event in August 2026. Brick Fanatics reported on 19 October 2026 a rumour, not confirmed by LEGO, that NINJAGO will get eight SMART Play sets in August 2027.
- **Search interest is climbing.** Rising Trends, which tracks search volumes, listed "Smart Brick" searches at around 47 times the level of a year earlier and up 124% over three months, in its October 2026 toy-trend report.
- **It is on Christmas lists.** Reviewed's 2026 holiday toy guide includes the Star Wars Throne Room Duel set in its "new for 2026" picks.
- **LEGO is investing in it.** LEGO's first-half 2026 results reported record revenue, up 21%, and named SMART Play as a new platform. LEGO has not published sales figures for SMART Play itself, so how well it is selling is not public.

## Who it is for

- **Children around 6 to 12** who like role-play with their models. LEGO rates the Pikachu set 6+ and the Throne Room set 9+. Blocks magazine lists the X-wing as 6+ and the TIE Fighter as 8+.
- **Star Wars and Pokémon fans**, since those are the only themes so far.
- **Families who want tech without a screen.** The brick works without a phone or tablet.

It is less suited to adult collectors who mainly display sets. Reviewers note that SMART Play sets cost more per piece than standard sets, and the extra money goes on electronics rather than model detail.

## Downsides and risks

- **You must buy a full set to get a brick.** LEGO does not sell Smart Bricks, tags or chargers separately, and Compatible sets do nothing electronic on their own.
- **Short play time per charge.** LEGO says about 45 minutes of play per charge. Reviewers report around two to two and a half hours to recharge.
- **The battery cannot be replaced.** A teardown reported by BGR found the battery is sealed in. LEGO has not said what happens when batteries wear out.
- **Not every sensor is switched on yet.** Testers found some sensors disabled in the first batch. LEGO says future updates and sets will enable more.
- **Sound quality divides people.** Several reviews call the synthesised voices and effects the weakest part.
- **Some play experts are wary.** Fairplay, a US children's advocacy group, told the BBC that electronics could undermine the imaginative play LEGO is known for.
- **There is a microphone.** LEGO says the brick's sound sensor cannot store or transmit sounds. More in [is LEGO SMART Play worth it?](/lego-smart-play/is-lego-smart-play-worth-it/)
- **Small parts.** LEGO's listings carry a choking-hazard warning: not for children under 3.

## How to get started

1. **Pick a theme your child already loves.** Star Wars or Pokémon is the only choice for now.
2. **Buy an All-in-One set first.** For most families the Pikachu Training House or the TIE Fighter is the cheapest way in. Both include one brick and the charger.
3. **Add Compatible sets later**, knowing they rely on the brick from the first set. A second brick only comes with another All-in-One set.
4. **Charge before the big day.** LEGO says the brick ships ready to use, but a full charge gives the most play.
5. **Install the app if you want volume control.** It is optional, and Stonewars found every setting works in guest mode, with no LEGO account needed.

A fully charged LEGO Smart Brick gives about **45 minutes of continuous lights and sounds**, according to LEGO's own product listing, and takes around **two to two and a half hours** to recharge on the wireless Smart Charger that comes with every All-in-One set. Only the brick needs power: Smart Tags and Smart Minifigures have no battery. The free LEGO SMART Assist app is optional; you only need it to change the volume, see an exact battery percentage or install updates.

## Battery life in practice

LEGO quotes about 45 minutes per charge, and Blocks magazine gave the same figure for nonstop use. Real sessions usually last longer than that, because the brick is not making noise the whole time a child is building or setting up a scene.

The brick shows its charge with coloured lights. LEGO lists four states: green, yellow, orange and red. For an exact percentage you need the app.

### Why the brick goes to sleep

The Smart Brick switches itself off after about three minutes without movement, to save power. BGR reported that if it has been asleep for more than about 13 hours, picking it up will not wake it: you have to put it back on the charger first. That catches people out on a first morning ("it's broken!"), so if a brick seems dead, try the charger before anything else.

## How charging works

Charging is wireless, using a coil, much like an electric toothbrush. You place the brick on the Smart Charger, which LEGO says can charge **two bricks at once**.

- **What's in the box:** every All-in-One set includes one charger, including the two-brick sets. Brickset's review of the Charizard set lists a yellow charging dock and a USB-A to USB-C cable.
- **What isn't:** a plug. The charger runs from a USB power source, so you need a USB plug adaptor or a computer port.
- **Replacements:** LEGO does not sell the charger on its own. Forbes noted in September 2026 that Pokémon and Star Wars bricks work on either charger. Used chargers have appeared on BrickLink from about £9 (March 2026 prices), but these are second-hand.

**Tip:** if you have two All-in-One sets, you have two chargers. Keep one with the toys and one somewhere safe.

## The SMART Assist app: do you need it?

No. The brick does not need an app to set up or play, which is a change from earlier LEGO tech lines such as Hidden Side and VIDIYO that relied on a phone. Stonewars, Blocks and Tom's Guide all confirm this.

The app, called **LEGO SMART Assist**, is aimed at parents. It is on the Apple App Store and Google Play. It can:

- **Change the volume.** Stonewars calls this, with the battery display, the app's main practical use. Reviewers often mention the sounds being shrill or chatty.
- **Show the exact battery percentage.**
- **Install firmware updates**, which can fix bugs, patch security issues and switch on new features.
- **Name bricks**, handy if siblings each have one.
- **Show help topics** for getting started.

Tom's Guide found that the app connects to the brick only while it sits on the charging pad, and that it adds no play features.

### Accounts and data

Stonewars found that all of the app's functions work in **guest mode**, without a LEGO account. The app asks whether you want to share usage data; saying no had no effect on what it could do in their testing.

### The microphone

The brick has a sound sensor that uses a microphone. LEGO says the brick "cannot store or transmit any sounds." Stonewars reported in March 2026 that the microphone is currently switched off, and that any future use would need consent through the app. If privacy is your main worry, the safest approach is to skip the app or decline any new permissions it asks for. None of LEGO's published specifications list a camera.

## Features that may arrive later

BGR reported that testers found some of the brick's sensors switched off in the first batch, and that LEGO says all of the expected sensors are physically present. LEGO's features page also lists a range sensor, so bricks can tell how far apart they are and in which direction, as "available with upcoming" sets. Updates through the app are how such features would be switched on. Nobody outside LEGO knows what effect extra sensors will have on battery life.

## Troubleshooting checklist

1. **Brick won't light up:** put it on the charger. A long sleep (over about 13 hours) needs the charger to wake it.
2. **Brick doesn't react to a set:** check the set is SMART Play. Compatible sets react only when a brick is added, and ordinary LEGO sets have no tags.
3. **Tag not detected:** move the tag or figure closer. Tom's Guide measured the reading range at roughly an inch.
4. **Too loud:** install the app and turn the volume down.
5. **Battery drains fast:** check it was fully charged. Charge time is two hours or more, so a short top-up gives less play.

LEGO SMART Play is worth it for a child aged roughly 6 to 12 who loves Star Wars or Pokémon and plays out stories with their models: reviewers broadly agree the Smart Brick adds real play value without a screen. It is poor value if you mainly want a model to display, because you pay a premium for electronics rather than extra bricks, the sounds are synthesised rather than film-accurate, and the sealed battery means the brick will not last forever. The cheapest sensible test is one All-in-One set at £59.99 / $69.99, often less at retailers.

## What reviewers like

- **It works without a screen or app.** Tom's Guide, Blocks and Stonewars all stress that play needs no phone or tablet, unlike earlier LEGO tech lines.
- **The play features are varied.** Brickset's reviewer called the Pikachu set a "shockingly adorable" starter, with training, feeding, capture and sleep reactions, and found it more engaging than the first Star Wars set at a similar price.
- **Motion and colour sensing make it feel responsive.** Tom's Guide liked that swooping the TIE Fighter or setting it down changes the sound, and that coloured studs trigger weapons, refuelling or repair effects.
- **The brick works with ordinary LEGO.** Smart Tags can be clipped onto existing sets, and Tom's Guide expects the brick to keep working even if LEGO stops making new SMART sets.
- **Owners rate it well.** Brickpicker collected an average owner rating of 4.4 out of 5 from 305 ratings for the Throne Room set, with an expert "BrickScore" of 72 (recommended) and playability its strongest score.

## What reviewers dislike

- **The sound.** This is the most common complaint. Brickset found the synthesised Pokémon voices jarring, Brickpicker's summary lists poor brick sounds as a con, and Brickset's Charizard review called the wing-flap sound annoying.
- **The price premium.** Brickset estimated that about £25 of the Pikachu set's £59.99 price is the electronics. Tom's Guide noted the SMART Millennium Falcon ($100) costs more than a standard, more detailed Falcon ($85). When fans complained, LEGO told Brick Fanatics that interactive play offers "a different value equation."
- **You can't buy a brick on its own.** LEGO sells bricks, chargers and tags only inside sets. Compatible sets are plain LEGO until you add a brick, which is easy to miss when buying gifts.
- **Short play per charge.** About 45 minutes of continuous use, then around two hours to recharge.
- **Builds can be simple.** Tom's Guide found the TIE Fighter an easy build for an adult; Brickpicker's summary said neither model in the Throne Room set feels full-sized.

## The battery question

The battery inside the Smart Brick **cannot be replaced**, according to a teardown reported by BGR, and LEGO has not said how it will handle batteries that wear out. LEGO does say the battery should keep working after years in storage. Rechargeable batteries generally lose capacity with use, so a brick that gets heavy daily play will run for less time each charge as the years go by. Nobody knows yet how quickly that will happen with this brick, since it has only been on sale since March 2026.

That matters for value. A normal LEGO set can be handed down for decades; the electronic part of a SMART set may not. The rest of the set is ordinary LEGO and keeps working without the brick.

When a brick or charger does reach the end of its life, it counts as electrical waste. Brickset found an e-waste leaflet in the box. Check your local council (UK) or city or state (US) rules for recycling small electricals rather than putting it in the bin.

## Privacy and safety

- **Microphone.** The brick has a sound sensor with a microphone. LEGO says it "cannot store or transmit any sounds." Stonewars reported that the microphone is currently switched off and that any future use would need consent through the app.
- **App and data.** The SMART Assist app is optional. Stonewars found every setting works in guest mode, without a LEGO account, and you can decline data sharing.
- **Connectivity.** Bricks talk to each other over a short-range Bluetooth-based link. LEGO's published specifications do not mention internet connectivity for the brick itself or any AI features.
- **Small parts.** LEGO's listing carries a choking-hazard warning: not for children under 3. Keep sets away from younger siblings.

The University of Edinburgh's Professor Andrew Manches told the BBC that smart toys in general raise security and privacy questions that deserve scrutiny, especially those using AI. SMART Play does not use AI as far as LEGO has disclosed, but the general advice holds: keep the firmware updated through the app and read any new permission it asks for.

## Is it bad for imaginative play?

This is a matter of opinion and there is no research on SMART Play itself yet. Josh Golin of Fairplay, a US children's advocacy group, told the BBC that the electronics could undermine what made LEGO great, since children already make their models move and talk in their imaginations. LEGO's Tom Donaldson, who runs its Creative Play Lab, said the technology is designed to respond to what children do rather than direct them. A Mumsnet parent guide, which is a paid advertisement feature, makes the same case: children still build and invent the story. Independent research on this specific toy has not been published yet.

If this worries you, one middle path is to buy a single All-in-One set and let the brick be one toy among many ordinary LEGO sets rather than the centre of every game.

## Who should buy, and who should skip

| Buy it if | Skip it if |
|---|---|
| Your child is 6 to 12 and loves Star Wars or Pokémon | You mainly want display models |
| They play out battles and stories with sets | You want the most bricks for the money |
| You want tech toys without a screen | You dislike toys that make noise |
| Two children can share a two-brick set (Charizard or Throne Room) | You expect a toy to last for decades unchanged |

## Cheaper ways to try it

1. **Start with one All-in-One set.** Pikachu or the TIE Fighter, both £59.99 / $69.99 at LEGO and often less at retailers. See [SMART Play sets and prices](/lego-smart-play/lego-smart-play-sets-and-prices/).
2. **Wait for a deal.** Amazon US had the Throne Room and other SMART sets around 20% off in October 2026.
3. **Consider a loose brick for an older child.** Brick Fanatics found used bricks on BrickLink from about £16 in March 2026, but you lose the set and cannot check battery wear.

Clickeez are small plastic collectables shaped like the keys on a computer keyboard. Each one has a cartoon character on top and makes the same click-clack sound as a mechanical keyboard when you press it. Kids clip them together into keychains, click them as fidget toys, and slot them into a toy keyboard case to show off their collection. They are made by the games company Goliath, launched in the US in July 2025, and by October 2026 they were in UK Christmas toy round-ups. Packs start at £4.99 in the UK and $4.99 in the US.

This page covers what they are, why interest has grown so quickly, who they suit, what they cost and the downsides worth knowing before a child starts collecting.

## What Clickeez are

Each Clickeez piece is a single "key" with a working switch inside, so it clicks when pressed. The top carries a character face, and pieces link together through connectors on the back, so you can build a chain of keys and hang it from a bag clip or lanyard. Goliath sells them in:

- **Blind packs** of 2, 5, 7 or 12, where some characters are visible through the packaging and the rest are hidden until you open it.
- **Keychains**, which give you a clip and a few keys.
- **The Keyboard Mega Pack**, a keyboard-shaped storage case with a slot for each character in a series, sold with 12 keys including three that light up.
- **An advent calendar**, listed by Smyths UK for Christmas 2026.

Every pack comes with a collector's guide showing the full set. Some characters are rarer than others, and Walmart's listing for a Series 3 five-pack says one piece is a limited edition, one of 20,000.

## Why interest is rising

Goliath's US president Brian Weiss told Entrepreneur in August 2026 that the idea came from adult trends: people in the company's own office were into customising mechanical keyboards, and the team combined that with the sensory and fidget-toy trend for children. Development took about a year.

The rise since then is well documented:

- **July 2025:** The first two-packs went on sale at the US discount chain Five Below at $4.99, with Amazon and Walmart following in late summer and autumn.
- **2025 to 2026:** Goliath says sales spread through TikTok, Reddit and word of mouth after only a small amount of advertising. The line won Collectible of the Year in France and was picked up by Smyths in the UK and Ireland.
- **August 2026:** Goliath said it had sold more than 10 million pieces and expected to pass 100 million by the end of 2026. That forecast is the company's own and has not been independently checked.
- **September 2026:** A US toy trend tracker listed Clickeez among the fidget toys driving millions of TikTok views.
- **October 2026:** Which? named a Clickeez mega mystery pack in The Entertainer's Christmas picks and said the brand went viral on TikTok and Instagram this year.

A sourcing blog writing in October 2026 also noted rivals entering the space, including the streamer-backed TapCaps, which is a sign the format has caught on beyond one brand. See our [Clickeez vs TapCaps comparison](/clickeez/clickeez-vs-tapcaps/).

## Who Clickeez are for

Goliath rates them for ages 6 and up. The packaging and retailer pages carry the standard small-parts choking warning and say they are not for children under 3, so they are not suitable for homes with toddlers who put things in their mouths unless they are kept out of reach.

They suit children who like:

- **Collecting and swapping**, since the guide and hidden pieces push kids to complete sets.
- **Fidget toys**, because the click is the main appeal. A reader comment on a gifted UK review said they are small enough to be fairly discreet in a classroom, but check your school's rules before sending them in.
- **Bag charms and keychains**, since every pack is built around clipping keys onto a chain.

## Downsides and risks

- **Blind packs add up.** A sourcing analysis estimated that collecting all 70 characters in one series without duplicates would cost around $120, and in practice duplicates make it more. Rarity tiers and limited editions are designed to make children want more packs.
- **Resale markups.** Marketplace sellers have listed the $29.99 keyboard for nearly double. Buy from mainstream retailers where you can.
- **Copycats.** Goliath has said copycat products have appeared. Lookalike keys may not carry the same safety testing. See [how to spot them](/clickeez/clickeez-price-guide/#copycats-and-fakes).
- **Small parts.** Not for under-3s, and loose keys are easy to lose. One reviewer found the connectors fiddly and advised against clipping lots to one bag charm.
- **Noise.** The click is the point, which not every parent or teacher will enjoy.
- **Fads fade.** Goliath is planning clothes, bags and bedding, but collectable crazes can drop off quickly. Treat it as a toy, not an investment.

## How to get started

1. **Start small.** A £4.99 or $4.99 two-pack shows whether your child likes the feel and the sound before you spend more.
2. **Pick one series.** Each series has its own guide and keyboard case slots, so focusing on one makes completing a set realistic.
3. **Decide on the keyboard later.** The Mega Pack is good value if a child is already hooked, since it includes 12 keys and storage, but it is the item most often marked up.
4. **Set a budget** for blind packs and agree on swapping duplicates with friends rather than buying more.
5. **Buy from known retailers** such as Smyths, Argos, Target, Walmart or Five Below. See [where to buy Clickeez](/clickeez/where-to-buy-clickeez/).

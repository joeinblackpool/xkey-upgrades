A genuine Clickeez two-pack should cost about £4.99 in the UK or $4.99 in the US, a five-pack £9.99 or $9.99, and the Keyboard Mega Pack £29.99 or $29.99. Those are Smyths' UK prices and Goliath's US launch prices. Marketplace sellers often charge far more: we found the $29.99 keyboard listed at $57.95 by a third-party seller on Walmart. Collecting a whole series from blind packs costs much more than the price of one pack suggests, because of duplicates.

*Prices checked 16 October 2026.* Sources are listed at the top of this page.

## What a full series costs

Each series has dozens of characters. Series 1 and Series 3 each have more than 70, and Series 2 added more than 30. Because some keys are hidden and some are rarer, you cannot buy a complete set directly.

A sourcing analysis published in October 2026 estimated that collecting all 70 characters in a series with no duplicates at all would cost around $120. That is a best case: real collections pick up duplicates, so the actual cost is usually higher. Swapping duplicates with friends is the cheapest way to fill gaps.

Rarer pieces push the cost up further. Walmart's Series 3 five-pack listing says each pack includes one limited-edition key, one of 20,000. We did not find reliable sold-price data for individual rare keys, so we cannot say what they fetch on resale. Treat any "rare" listing price with caution.

Clickeez are not a sound investment. Goliath is making them in huge volumes: it said in August 2026 that it had sold more than 10 million pieces and expected to pass 100 million by the end of the year. Mass production on that scale tends to keep ordinary pieces cheap.

## Copycats and fakes

Goliath's Brian Weiss has said copycat products have appeared since Clickeez took off. Some are open lookalikes with different names; others may copy the branding. Neither has to meet the same testing as the real thing.

UK officials have already warned about fake versions of other toy crazes. The Intellectual Property Office said in October 2025 that three in four seized counterfeit toys failed critical safety tests, with risks including choking hazards and banned chemicals. In July 2026 Carmarthenshire Trading Standards warned that fake squishy toys were missing safety markings and importer details. We have not seen an official warning about fake Clickeez specifically, but the same checks apply.

### How to check a Clickeez pack

- **Brand and maker.** Clickeez is a Goliath brand, so the pack should name Clickeez and give the maker's details. Similar-looking keys under another name are not Clickeez, whatever the listing says.
- **Collector's guide.** Every genuine pack we found listed includes a series guide. A pack without one is a warning sign.
- **Safety warning.** Genuine listings carry a small-parts choking warning saying they are not for children under 3.
- **UKCA or CE mark and an address.** In the UK, look for a UKCA or CE mark and a UK contact address, as the IPO advises. In the US, PIRG notes that children's products must show their country of origin.
- **Price.** A keyboard or big multipack well below RRP from an unknown seller is a red flag, just as a big markup is.
- **Seller.** Buy from the retailer itself rather than a marketplace seller where possible, and read the negative reviews.
- **Scan the barcode.** PIRG suggests scanning the UPC or QR code; it should lead back to the product or company.

If you find a fake or unsafe toy in the UK, the IPO's advice is to stop using it, return it, report the seller to the platform and contact Trading Standards via the Citizens Advice consumer helpline on 0808 223 1133. In the US you can report unsafe products to the Consumer Product Safety Commission at saferproducts.gov.

When someone posts the nihilist penguin, they are usually saying "I'm done, I'm walking away", or "I'm going my own way, even if it's hard". The clip shows a single Adélie penguin turning its back on its colony and the sea and walking towards mountains it can't survive in. People use it as shorthand for burnout, wanting to be alone, quitting the crowd, or quiet determination. The label "nihilist" is the internet's joke, not something Herzog or any scientist said. The real bird was most likely disoriented, as the [science page](/nihilistic-penguin/why-do-penguins-walk-away/) explains.

## The main meanings

The meme gets used in very different moods. Reports from USA Today, Storyboard18 and the Bangladeshi news agency UNB describe roughly the same set:

| Meaning | What the post is saying | Typical tone |
|---|---|---|
| Burnout | "I'm leaving my job, my inbox and everyone" | Tired, funny |
| Introvert / overstimulated | "Too many people, I need to go stand on a mountain" | Light, relatable |
| Going your own way | Choosing the "road not taken" over a 9-to-5 routine | Earnest, motivational |
| Existential | Life is pointless, so why not walk towards the mountains | Dark, deadpan |
| Curiosity | Heading into the unknown, the way explorers do | Hopeful |

USA Today's 27 January 2026 explainer found people both relating to the penguin and being inspired by it. One TikTok user it quoted said the bird might just be an introvert who wants to be left alone. Storyboard18 read it as a mix of defying conformity, burnout and alienation. UNB noted that many people who feel stuck or drained see themselves in the bird.

### Why "nihilist"?

The name comes from early YouTube uploads. According to Know Your Meme, a 2015 upload titled "Nihilist Penguin" became the most-watched early version of the clip. Earlier uploads called it "Deranged Penguin". Nihilism, the idea that life has no inherent meaning, fits the bleak reading: the bird seems to choose a pointless walk to nowhere. The "be that penguin" version flips this, so the walk becomes a brave choice rather than a hopeless one. Both readings use the same footage.

## Common captions and formats

Know Your Meme catalogues these as the main formats from January 2026:

- **"But why?"** Herzog's line from the narration, used as on-screen text or as the whole joke.
- **"Be that penguin" / "Be the damn penguin".** Motivational edits, often with travel or mountain footage. A Scottish Highlands clip with the second caption passed 3.3 million likes.
- **Life-question setups.** For example, "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" followed by the penguin walking off.
- **Reaction GIFs.** Posted in comment sections, with lines like "brb just pondering that damn penguin".
- **Quote edits.** The clip overlaid with Nietzsche or other philosophical quotes.
- **Trolley problem and AI edits.** Jokes, AI-generated versions and game-themed versions (Know Your Meme mentions a Skyrim one).

UNB reported one caption that spread widely: a bird that cannot fly, walking towards the mountains in the hope of touching the sky.

According to the *TikTok TikTok TikTok* newsletter, there is also a "hero edit" format. Creators cut the penguin together with figures such as Joan of Arc, Alexander the Great, Aragorn or Luke Skywalker, set to the organ music, and many of these reached millions of views.

## Sounds and music

There are two sounds people use with the clip:

1. **Herzog's own narration.** His calm voice saying the bird is heading for the mountains, followed by "But why?". On its own it plays as wry or melancholy.
2. **The organ "L'Amour Toujours".** An organ cover of Gigi D'Agostino's 1999 Eurodance track, recorded by organist Andreas Gärtner and uploaded on 17 January 2023. It went viral on its own before the penguin did. Know Your Meme dates the first edit combining it with Herzog's narration to 16 January 2026, and calls the pairing the trend's two core elements.

Some coverage, including Storyboard18 and USA Today, only describes "moody" or "instrumental" music without naming a track. Many edits use other sounds.

### Why the music matters

The organ track carries baggage. *TikTok TikTok TikTok* reports that from late 2023, far-right groups in Germany and elsewhere sang racist chants to "L'Amour Toujours", including during a widely reported incident on Sylt. The newsletter says organisers responded by banning the song at Oktoberfest. It argues that the serious tone of the organ, Herzog's voice and the footage of heroic figures gave the meme an earnest, civilisation-defending feel, and that this made it easy to use as a coded signal.

Writing for The Loop, the European Consortium for Political Research's blog, Federico Taddei describes alt-right edits that link the penguin to "Western decline", Christian nationalism, nostalgia for authoritarian regimes and coded symbols. He argues that apolitical memes are easy to repurpose and that algorithms amplify the results.

These are commentators' interpretations. Most people sharing the penguin mean nothing political. Still, as both writers point out, a format can carry a meaning its sharer never intended. If you want the joke without the ambiguity, Herzog's narration alone is the safer sound.

## Is it the same as other penguin memes?

Not quite. *TikTok TikTok TikTok* links it to an earlier viral clip in which a small boy, asked who says men can't wear make-up, points at a paper penguin on the wall. That penguin became a tongue-in-cheek "defender of manhood". The newsletter suggests this helped give penguins a masculine, ironic edge online before Herzog's bird took off. The White House's "Embrace the penguin" image of 23 January 2026 is a separate, political spin-off, covered on the [hub page](/nihilistic-penguin/).

## A note on the real penguin

In the film, Herzog asks penguin scientist David Ainley whether penguins can go insane. As Distractify reports it, Ainley says he has never seen that, but that penguins do get disoriented. The meme's meanings belong to people, not the bird. For the original scene see [where the clip comes from](/nihilistic-penguin/nihilist-penguin-original-clip/).

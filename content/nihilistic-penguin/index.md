The "nihilist penguin" (also called the nihilistic penguin, lonely penguin or "Herzog's penguin") is a short clip of a single Adélie penguin walking away from its colony and the sea, heading inland towards distant Antarctic mountains. It comes from Werner Herzog's 2007 documentary *Encounters at the End of the World*. The clip had been online since 2008, but in January 2026 it spread rapidly as a meme template on TikTok and Instagram. Most people use it to talk about burnout, wanting to be alone or walking away from a life that isn't working. It was also picked up by the White House, by brands and by far-right accounts, and that use has caused arguments.

## What happens in the clip

Late in the film, Herzog visits a penguin colony near McMurdo Station, the US research base in Antarctica. Most of the birds are heading to the sea to feed. One stops, stands still for a moment, then turns and walks towards the mountains. In his narration Herzog says the mountains are about 70 kilometres away. He also says that if the bird were caught and carried back to the colony, it would set straight off for the mountains again. Then he asks, "But why?"

USA Today places the scene about an hour and 13 minutes into the film. Herzog presents the walk as a journey towards certain death. Nobody filmed what happened to the bird afterwards. The [original clip guide](/nihilistic-penguin/nihilist-penguin-original-clip/) has more on the scene and on David Ainley, the penguin scientist Herzog interviews.

| 19–21 Jan 2026 | "Be that penguin" motivational edits spread; one Scottish Highlands version passes 3.3 million likes |
| 23 Jan 2026 | The White House and the US Department of Homeland Security post penguin images on X |

Mainstream coverage followed within days. Storyboard18 (India) wrote about it on 23–24 January. USA Today explained it on 27 January. The Bangladeshi news agency UNB's piece was carried by Malaysia's Bernama on 26 January. Bernama's report says the clip had built up millions of views, but none of these reports gives a reliable total, so we don't quote one.

## What people use it to say

The meme has two moods, and you need to know both before you use it.

- **Walking away.** The penguin stands in for anyone who is burnt out, overstimulated or tired of a 9-to-5 life and wants to leave it. Some posts play this as a joke (he's "just an introvert"), others play it straight.
- **Going your own way.** "Be that penguin" edits use the clip as motivation to quit the crowd and head for something harder. These are often set to the organ music and paired with mountain footage or Nietzsche quotes.

Detailed formats and captions are on the [meaning page](/nihilistic-penguin/what-does-nihilist-penguin-mean/).

## The political appropriation debate

The meme's most common soundtrack is an organ cover of Gigi D'Agostino's 1999 track "L'Amour Toujours". According to the Substack newsletter *TikTok TikTok TikTok*, far-right groups in Germany and elsewhere have attached racist chants to that tune since late 2023. Its highest-profile use was an incident on Sylt.

The meme's political use has been reported in three ways:

- **US government accounts.** On 23 January 2026 the White House posted an AI-generated image on X of Donald Trump walking across ice with a penguin carrying a US flag, captioned "Embrace the penguin". It was tied to the administration's push to acquire Greenland. Newsweek reported mockery from several critics, who pointed out that penguins do not live in Greenland. The DHS X account posted a version captioned "Americans have always known 'why'".
- **Far-right edits.** In a March 2026 article for The Loop, the European Consortium for Political Research's blog, University of Milan PhD candidate Federico Taddei argues that alt-right accounts recast the penguin as a symbol of resistance to "Western decline". He reports that some edits added Christian-nationalist themes, nostalgia for authoritarian regimes and coded extremist symbols such as the Black Sun.
- **Brands.** The Substack piece reports that Lidl posted a TikTok using the Herzog and organ footage on 27 January 2026, and that Sixt and Deichmann used the meme too. It argues that brand and government use spread a far-right-coded format without most viewers noticing.

These are arguments made by commentators, not settled facts. Most people posting the penguin are joking about their job, not signalling anything. Still, if you post it with the organ track, some viewers will read a meaning you didn't intend.

## Merch and products

Print-on-demand sites quickly started selling designs based on the clip. One example is a TeePublic listing for "BUT WHY?" shorts filed under "Nihilist Penguin", priced at $21 (reduced from $27) when we checked. These are independent sellers' designs, not official products. We found no officially licensed merchandise. The film was produced by Discovery Films and ThinkFilm.

## How to use it and where to find the original

1. **Watch the scene in context first.** The full film adds a lot: the scientist interview before the walk and Herzog's deadpan narration. In the US, JustWatch listed no streaming option when we checked, only DVD and Blu-ray to buy. The [clip guide](/nihilistic-penguin/nihilist-penguin-original-clip/) has the details.
2. **Choose your sound deliberately.** Herzog's voice on its own reads as wry or sad. The organ "L'Amour Toujours" reads as epic, and to some viewers it reads as political.
3. **Keep the caption short.** Most successful versions use one line: "But why?", "Be that penguin", or a relatable situation.
4. **Remember the real bird.** Scientists see this behaviour as disorientation, not a choice. The [science page](/nihilistic-penguin/why-do-penguins-walk-away/) explains what's known.

## What we couldn't confirm

Some viral posts and news pieces say experts "confirmed" the penguin died after walking about 70 km. Herzog's narration says it was heading to its death, but we found no record that anyone saw what happened next, so we treat its fate as unknown.

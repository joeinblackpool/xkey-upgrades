Stickerbox: the AI voice-to-sticker printer explained

What Stickerbox is, how the AI sticker printer works, what it costs in the US, why it is hard to get in the UK, and the privacy and paper downsides.

Stickerbox is a small red box that turns a child's spoken idea into a printed sticker. The child holds down a big white button, says something like "a lizard riding a skateboard", and a few seconds later a black-and-white sticker comes out of the top, ready to colour in. It is made by Hapiko, a startup in Brooklyn, New York, and costs $129.99 on the official US site. It is not officially sold in the UK.

This page covers how it works, why so many people have been talking about it, who it suits, what it really costs to run, and the downsides worth knowing before you buy.

How it works

The box is a 3.75-inch cube with a small black-and-white screen, one large push-to-talk button and a thermal printer inside. According to TechCrunch and Yanko Design, the steps are:

  1. A parent connects the box to Wi-Fi using a companion phone app (you scan a QR code on the screen and enter a code).
  2. The child holds the button and describes a picture out loud, then lets go.
  3. The words appear on screen as text. The request goes over Wi-Fi to AI image models, which produce simple line art.
  4. The thermal printer burns the image onto sticker paper. No ink is needed.
  5. The child peels, colours and sticks.

Stickers are black and white on purpose, so children colour them in; the starter set comes with eight coloured pencils. Hapiko's FAQ says the box understands several languages. It has no battery: it only works when plugged in, and it needs Wi-Fi or a phone hotspot to make new stickers.

TechCrunch reported that Stickerbox uses a mix of AI models plus Hapiko's own safety technology, and TechRadar named image models from Ideogram and Flux. Hapiko's own pages that we read do not name the models.

Who makes it

Hapiko was founded in 2025 by Arun Gupta (chief executive) and Robert "Bob" Whitney (chief technology officer), who previously worked together at the clothing marketplace Grailed. TechCrunch reports that the idea came from Whitney's son asking for a picture of a lizard on a skateboard after Whitney had made him a printable image with ChatGPT.

Why interest is rising

  • Launch and funding (November 2025). Hapiko announced Stickerbox on 18 November 2025 alongside a $7 million funding round led by Maveron and Serena Ventures, the fund started by Serena Williams. It launched at $99.99, US only.
  • Early sell-outs (November 2025). A Tom's Guide piece, last updated 18 November 2025, described it as selling out fast.
  • Viral posts (early 2026). TechRadar wrote in January 2026 that TikTok users, many of them adults, were posting odd prompts and results, and followed up in February 2026 with a hands-on piece calling it the kids' toy adults were obsessed with.
  • Prime Day (June 2026). Tom's Guide reported on 24 June 2026 that Stickerbox was 20% off on Amazon for Prime Day, $103 instead of $129, and said it had rarely been discounted.
  • Repeated stock-outs (August 2026). The Everymom's August 2026 review said the toy had sold out 13 times.
  • Holiday lists (October 2026). TODAY's list of the hottest toys of 2026, published 14 October 2026, included Stickerbox at $129.99 for ages 5 and up.

Who it is for

Hapiko says Stickerbox is designed for children aged 5 and up, and that 2- to 4-year-olds can use it with help from an adult or older sibling. Reviewers broadly back this up: one Tom's Guide writer found it simple enough for a 4-year-old and interesting enough for an 11-year-old, while The Everymom's reviewer said the box sometimes struggled to understand her 3-year-old. A Tom's Guide writer also noted it misheard a prompt from her son, who has a speech delay.

It suits children who like drawing, colouring and stickers, and parents who want a creative toy without a tablet screen. It is less suited to families without reliable home Wi-Fi, and to anyone hoping for colour prints or photo stickers.

There is no subscription. The companion app is used for set-up, viewing past creations and reprinting. Hapiko's FAQ says each roll gives 66 stickers, so a standard refill works out at roughly 3 cents a sticker. Running costs depend on use: The Everymom's reviewer said her child made 1,273 stickers in nine months, which at 66 per roll is about 20 rolls, or roughly $40 of standard paper.

Downsides and risks

It only uses its own paper

Hapiko's FAQ says only official Stickerbox paper is supported, and other thermal paper will damage the printer and void the warranty. That ties you to one supplier, and refills have been listed as sold out at times.

It needs Wi-Fi and a socket

No Wi-Fi, no new stickers, so it will not work in the car. TechRadar's reviewer had to reconnect to Wi-Fi several times in early use, and The Everymom's reviewer said it needs reconnecting when moved to a new location.

AI does not always get it right

Prompts can be misheard or turned into something different from what the child imagined, and every sticker prints without a preview, so misses use up paper.

Filtering is good but not something you can inspect

Hapiko says every prompt passes through several safety layers and unsuitable requests produce nothing, with the child asked to try another idea. Reviewers say violence, sexual content, swearing and brand names such as Disney are blocked. These are informal tests, not an independent audit.

Voice data

Hapiko says the box only listens while the button is held. Its privacy policy (effective 17 April 2026) says children's audio is deleted immediately after the transcript and image are made, but transcripts and images are kept until a parent deletes them or the account. The policy does not mention UK data protection law. Details are in Is Stickerbox safe for kids?.

Thermal paper

Hapiko says its paper is free of BPA and BPS, two chemicals used in some thermal paper. That is the company's claim; we did not find independent test results.

Lookalikes in the UK

Because the real thing is not sold in the UK, similar-looking "AI voice printers" from other makers turn up on UK deal sites. One on Wowcher in October 2026 was £29.99 from a different seller. These are different products with their own apps, paper and privacy terms. See Stickerbox alternatives.

How to get started

  1. Check you have 2.4GHz home Wi-Fi; Hapiko recommends a 2.4GHz network for set-up.
  2. Buy from the official site or the official Amazon listing if you are in the US. In the UK, read the where to buy guide first.
  3. Order a spare paper refill at the same time so you are not caught out by stock-outs.
  4. Set it up yourself with the app, then look through the privacy settings and creation history together with your child.
  5. Show your child that not every idea will work and that some prompts will be refused.

Clickeez vs TapCaps and other clicky keyboard toys

How Goliath's Clickeez compare with streamer-made TapCaps, handmade keycap keychains and squishies on price, age, rarity, keyboard use and where to buy.

Updated 2026-10-16

  4. Other alternatives

Clickeez and TapCaps both turn a single clicky keyboard key into a collectable character, but they are aimed at different buyers. Clickeez are a mass-market children's toy from Goliath, rated 6+, sold in shops in the UK and US from £4.99 or $4.99 for two. TapCaps are made by the gaming streamers Clix and Lacy, sold online in mystery boxes at $11.99 for one, and use a real keyboard switch, so they also fit many mechanical keyboards. For a younger child who wants to collect and clip keys to a bag, Clickeez are cheaper and easier to buy. For a teenager or gamer who wants something for their actual keyboard, TapCaps are closer to the mark.

Clickeez: strengths and weaknesses

Good for: children aged around 6 to 12 who like collecting, fidgeting and bag charms.

  • Cheap way in at £4.99 or $4.99, and easy to find in high-street toy shops.
  • Big ranges with collector's guides and a dedicated keyboard case for display.
  • Licensed Hello Kitty and Care Bears sets for fans of those characters.
  • TTPM's reviewer gave Series 2 five stars and liked the tactile clicking and unboxing.

Watch out for:

  • Blind packs mean duplicates and a high cost to finish a series. See the price guide.
  • One UK reviewer found the connectors hard to fit together and keep together, and warned that keys clipped to a bag are easy to lose.
  • They are a toy, not a keyboard accessory. The coverage we found does not say they fit real keyboards.

TapCaps: strengths and weaknesses

Good for: older children, teens and adults who already have a mechanical keyboard or follow the streamers.

  • A real Gateron blue keyboard switch inside each figure, rather than a toy click.
  • Can be mounted on an MX-style keyboard as a keycap or used on its own.
  • Small first wave of seven designs, which makes a set easier to complete in theory.

Watch out for:

  • At $11.99 for one piece, each TapCap costs more than a Clickeez five-pack. Some fans called it overpriced at launch, and esports.gg pointed out that a full keycap set of 100+ pieces can cost about $30.
  • One piece per box and a 1-in-102 pull rate for the rarest design mean chasing a full set gets expensive. Critics also said the format encourages resale.
  • Sold through the official website rather than shops. Dot Esports says international shipping is available, but we did not find a UK price, so UK buyers should allow for shipping and possible import charges.
  • The first drop sold out on the official site in under 24 hours, and a sourcing blog reported later drops selling thousands of units within minutes, so availability comes in bursts.

Other alternatives

Handmade keycap keychains

Independent makers on Etsy sell keycap fidget keychains. A sourcing analysis in October 2026 found them priced roughly $5 to $22. The upside is you can choose exactly what you get, with no blind packs. The downside is that quality and safety testing vary by seller, so they are better for teens and adults than young children.

Squishies and other fidget toys

If the appeal is mainly sensory, squishy toys are the other big fidget trend of 2026, and topped one tracker's TikTok toy views in September. They are quiet, which may suit classrooms better, but they come with their own fake-product problems. See our guide to dumpling squishies.

Mechanical keyboard keycap sets

For a teen who already has a mechanical keyboard, a full keycap set is often better value than collectable single keys, but it is a hobby purchase rather than a toy.

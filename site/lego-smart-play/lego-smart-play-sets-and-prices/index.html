LEGO SMART Play sets and prices in the UK and US

Every LEGO SMART Play set with UK and US prices, which ones include a Smart Brick, the cheapest way in, and what deals and loose bricks cost.

Updated 2026-10-21

On this page

  5. Is SMART Play expensive?

There are 20 LEGO SMART Play sets on sale as of October 2026: eight Star Wars and twelve Pokémon. Only five include a Smart Brick, and those are the ones to buy first: Training House with Pikachu and Darth Vader's TIE Fighter (both £59.99 / $69.99), Luke's Red Five X-wing ($89.99 in the US), Charizard vs. Jolteon Ultimate Battle (£109.99 / $119.99) and Throne Room Duel & A-Wing (£139.99 / $159.99). The other 15 are "Compatible" sets that contain smart tags and figures but need a brick from one of those five to light up or make sounds.

Prices are LEGO's own, checked on lego.com UK and US on 21 October 2026, unless another source and date is given. Retailers often sell for less.

Which All-in-One set to choose

  • Cheapest single brick: Pikachu or the TIE Fighter. Brickset's reviewer found the Pikachu set more varied in play than the TIE Fighter at a similar price.
  • Best value for two players: the Charizard set. It is the cheapest way to get two Smart Bricks from LEGO, which lets two children battle, and the two-brick sets are where multi-brick play comes in.
  • Biggest Star Wars set: the Throne Room. It also has two bricks, plus three smart minifigures (Luke, Vader and Palpatine).

LEGO.com limits the Throne Room to five per household and the Pikachu set to three, a sign it expects strong demand.

Watch out for "Compatible" on gift lists

Mewtwo's Lab Break costs the same as the Pikachu All-in-One set in both countries (£59.99 / $69.99) but has no Smart Brick. A child who unwraps only Compatible sets will get no lights or sounds. Check the box or listing for the words "All-in-One" before buying a first set.

Can you buy a Smart Brick on its own?

Not from LEGO. The brick, tags and charger are only sold inside sets, and Forbes reported in September 2026 that the charger is not sold separately either. Pokémon bricks work on the Star Wars charger and vice versa.

The workaround is the resale market. Within a week of launch, Brick Fanatics found loose Smart Bricks on BrickLink, a LEGO parts marketplace, from about £16, and chargers from about £9, so roughly £25 plus postage for both. You also need a standard USB-A to USB-C cable. That is cheaper than a £59.99 set, but you get no build, figures or tags, and buying used electronics means you cannot tell how worn the battery is. Prices were early-launch figures and will have moved.

Is SMART Play expensive?

Compared with ordinary LEGO, yes. Brickset worked out the Charizard set at 14.6p or 16 cents per piece, and Tom's Guide pointed out that the SMART Play Millennium Falcon cost more than a standard Falcon model with more detail. Brickset's reviewer estimated the electronics account for about £25 of the Pikachu set's £59.99 price. You are paying for the brick, not extra bricks.
